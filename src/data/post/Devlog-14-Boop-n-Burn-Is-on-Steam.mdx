It's finally on Steam!

**Boop n Burn** has its own store page, and you can wishlist it [right here](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2988610/). I've been sat on this one for a few weeks, so I'm glad I can finally just tell you.

If you're new round here, a quick word on wishlists, because they matter a lot more than people think. When you wishlist a game, Steam lets you know the moment it comes out or goes on sale. And the more wishlists a game has, the more Steam puts it in front of other people. For a little studio like us, that's massive. It's free and takes a couple of clicks, so if you like the look of it, I'd really appreciate it.

### **So What Is Boop n Burn?**

If you've not read the earlier devlogs, here's the idea. Up to **sixteen players** get dropped into an arena, and the lava rises the whole round. There's no health bar, and no respawns. You stay out of the lava and you shove everyone else into it. Last one standing wins the round, and first to three wins the match.

Pretty much everything comes down to the shove. **The faster you're running, the harder you hit**, so a full-speed tackle sends people flying, while a little tap from standing still barely moves them. You can climb walls, double jump and air dash, but they all use stamina, so you can't just camp on top of a wall all round. And if someone catches you mid-climb, you're coming off it.

### **Seven Arenas**

There are seven arenas in the game, and each one has its own gimmick on top of the lava:

- **The Yard**: shipping containers at sunset, with a crane moving the stacks around while you're stood on them.
- **Free Fall**: the cargo hold of a plane that's going down. The cargo breaks loose and slides out the back, and you go with it if you're in the way.
- **Puck Off**: an air hockey table in an old arcade. There's barely any grip, so once someone boops you, you're going wherever they sent you.
- **Dark Shoves**: a fortress full of swinging axes, gates and trapdoors that open under your feet.
- **Tipping Point**: one giant plate balanced on a pivot, and whoever's stood on it decides which way it tips.
- **Chain Reaction**: bombs that set each other off in lines across the floor, with blocks to duck behind.
- **Broadside**: a pirate fleet on rolling waves of lava. The ships sink and rise on the swell, the gangplanks snap if they drift too far apart, and the cannons actually launch people now.

There used to be twenty, believe it or not. The other thirteen were experiments: early test levels, a load of town layouts and a couple of big hand-built ones. None of them were anywhere near as good as these seven, and they'd been hidden from the menu for months anyway. A couple of weeks ago I finally bit the bullet and deleted them, and about **5,000 lines of code** and over **a gigabyte** of art went with them. It's a bit sad seeing that much work go, but the game's better for it.

### **Making the Store Page Out of the Game**

I set myself one rule for the store page: everything on it had to come out of the actual game. I didn't want a lovely painted capsule of something you'll never see when you play. If it's on the store page, it's in the game.

It started with a sketch from **Scott**, with the logo in the top left, someone flying through the air below it and the shover stood on a container on the right.

The finished version is set up inside The Yard. We built a tower out of the arena's own shipping containers, posed each character using frames from their own animations, and rendered the whole thing at double the size Steam asks for. The outlines, speed lines and the big impact burst went on afterwards, so they always line up between the fist and the face.

The victim is a clown, by the way, because a face still reads when the image is tiny. Plus a clown getting booped into lava doesn't really need explaining.

Making the art actually turned up a proper bug. One of the people who gave us feedback said the lava looked "low-res", like a badly compressed video. I put that down to the camera being too close. It wasn't. The maths behind the lava's pattern falls apart the further you get from the middle of the map, and it was breaking the surface up into hard-edged tiles. It had been doing that in every arena the whole time. Nothing had ever been close enough to the lava for anyone to notice. It's fixed now, and the lava looks a lot nicer up close.

### **Cutting the Trailer**

The trailer has had a few goes. The first cut opened on four seconds of our studio logo on a black screen, which I thought was fine. Then I found out Steam plays trailers with the sound off, and most people decide in about three seconds whether to keep watching. So now it opens straight on someone getting launched into the lava, and our logo's moved to the end where it belongs.

A few other things came up while I was on it. For the first 24 seconds, the trailer only showed The Yard, which is rusty red containers on orange lava. Shrink that down to a thumbnail and it's basically one colour, while five other arenas sat waiting their turn. Moving the shots round sorted that out. The sparks were a pain too. Every shove throws them, and with the glow turned up, each one was a big white blob right on top of the two characters you're meant to be watching. So oddly enough, it looked better once I turned the glow down.

The screenshots got the same treatment. We pulled 165 frames from real matches to pick the 11 on the store page, and nearly every keeper was taken a few frames *before* the hit. Just after it, the person getting booped has already flown out of shot.

### **Bots That Play Like People**

I'll talk about the bots properly next time, but this one's too good to leave out.

A playtest note came back saying the bots kept getting stuck trying to climb into overhangs. You could watch one wedge its head under a ledge, hang there doing nothing, drop off, then walk back and try the exact same wall again. So I measured it across six arenas, and every single stuck climb was a bot with its head against something. Now a bot checks what's above it before it climbs, and if there's a ledge in the way, it shuffles sideways round it like a player would. Bots used to spend **3 to 8 seconds a minute** stuck under ledges. Now it's **zero**.

I want the bots to feel like other players, so they play by the same rules as you. When I noticed bots could rest on a wall to get their stamina back and players couldn't, I didn't take it off the bots. I let players do it too.

### **The Lobby, the Characters and 27 Achievements**

The game used to have a little 3D town as its lobby, and you started a match by walking onto a glowing pad. It looked worse than the rest of the game, and having to walk somewhere just to start playing was a bit daft. So that's gone, and there's a proper lobby screen instead. Your party stands in a line, the match rules are on the side, and START is one button away. Couch players can join on their own controller and change their character without holding anyone else up.

### **The Demo and Next Fest**

There's a **free demo** coming in the new year, with six of the seven arenas. Broadside is staying back for the full game. I'll share the date in the next few weeks, and we're aiming to be part of **Steam Next Fest in February**.

The demo's already been on a bit of a diet, mind. The first build came out at **1.8 GB**, which is a lot for a party game people are going to try on a whim. It turned out it was bundling every prop from every art pack we own, including packs that no arena even uses. So I had the game play through every arena with a full lobby of bots and keep a list of every file it loaded. Out of about 30,000 files, it needed roughly **3,450**. The demo's now around **0.9 GB**, and nothing you can actually see or play in it was taken out.
